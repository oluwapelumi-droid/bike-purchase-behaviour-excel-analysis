# One-page executive brief

**Subject:** Which customer groups should a hypothetical bicycle retailer test first?

**Dataset:** 1,000 unique customer records after removing 26 exact duplicates from 1,026 source records. **481** customers purchased a bicycle (**48.1%**).

## Three findings that matter

**1. Commute distance:** Customers traveling 2–5 miles purchased at **58.6%** (95/162), versus **29.7%** (33/111) among those traveling 10+ miles. The largest *absolute* pool of buyers, however, came from the 0–1-mile group: **200 buyers**.

**2. Car ownership:** People with no cars purchased at **61.1%** (151/247), compared with **36.3%** (124/342) among those with two cars. This supports a hypothesis that a bicycle may substitute for some car trips.

**3. Age and region:** The 31–53 age band purchased at **54.3%** (372/685). Pacific had the highest regional *rate* (**58.9%**) while North America contributed the most regional *buyers* (**220**).

## Recommended tests

1. Pilot short-commute messaging, measure incremental conversion vs a control group.
2. Test “alternative mobility” positioning among customers with zero or one car.
3. Evaluate regional campaigns on both purchase rate **and** expected audience volume; compare cost per acquisition.

## Decision needed

Approve a **small controlled pilot** before allocating significant budget. The data suggests where to test; it does **not** justify a firm revenue forecast or prove what causes purchases.

## Critical caveat

This is an educational dataset, not a real retailer dataset. It lacks purchase dates, prices, marketing spend, and profit. Income currency is unknown. Recommendations are directional only.
